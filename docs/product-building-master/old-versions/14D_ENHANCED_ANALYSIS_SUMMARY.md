demonstrate at home.

Root causes likely include: Teaching interactive in class but feedback 
not visible to families, or students engaged but not demonstrating

If low, professional development in student-centered pedagogy is the 
priority. Expected impact: moving from 65 to 75 requires 70% of 
lessons showing active engagement."

## 📊 Analysis Components

### 1. DETAILED ANALYSIS (What does this score mean?)

**Four Score Ranges, Each with Specific Interpretation**:

| Score Range | Label | Analysis Focus |
|-------------|-------|-----------------|
| 85-100 | Excellent - Excellence | Maintain excellence; document practices; share as model |
| 70-85 | Strong - Performing Well | Solid performance; opportunity to strengthen further |
| 50-70 | Adequate - On Track | Near benchmark; functional but needs focus to improve |
| 30-50 | Poor - Needs Attention | Below benchmark; gaps evident; focused intervention needed |
| 0-30 | Critical - At Risk | Crisis level; requires immediate intensive action |

**For Each Range**:
- What the score indicates about school performance
- Specific indicators evident at that level
- What this means for students and school operations
- Stakeholder perception patterns (if divergent)

---

### 2. PERCEPTION-REALITY ANALYSIS (How accurate is stakeholder perception?)

**Three Gap Types Identified**:

1. **Alignment** (Within ±5 points)
   - Stakeholders see performance accurately
   - Realistic perception enables effective problem-solving
   - Focus: Maintain open communication

2. **Overestimation** (Stakeholders rate higher than data shows)
   - Risk: Complacency and missed improvement opportunities
   - Recommendation: Honest data sharing and dialogue
   - Action: Show concrete evidence to recalibrate expectations

3. **Underestimation** (Stakeholders rate lower than data shows)
   - Opportunity: Good work may be invisible to stakeholders
   - Recommendation: Better communication and visibility
   - Action: Highlight and celebrate actual performance

**Gap Analysis Includes**:
- Specific magnitude of gap
- What it suggests about perception vs. reality
- Root cause of the gap (visibility vs. operational)
- How to address it

---

### 3. ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS (Why is this performance level happening?)

**For Each Dimension**: Specific root cause categories

**Example (Academic Excellence)**:
- Systemic Deficiencies: Teaching method diversity, engagement strategies
- Resource Constraints: Materials, professional development, planning time
- Leadership/Vision Gap: Accountability, visibility, feedback systems
- Skill/Knowledge Gaps: Teacher training in pedagogy
- External Factors: Student mobility, home support, community expectations

**Analysis**:
- Lists probable root causes ranked by likelihood at that score level
- Provides diagnostic questions for stakeholders
- Distinguishes operational problems from visibility/communication issues
- Guides investigation strategy

---

### 4. ACTIONABLE RECOMMENDATIONS (What should we do?)

**Structured by Score Level and Timeline**:

**For Strong/Excellent Scores (75+)**:
1. Document practices and create case studies
2. Use as model for weaker dimensions
3. Establish peer mentoring relationships
4. Continue professional development
5. Annual sustainability assessment

**For Below-Benchmark Scores (Below 50)**:
1. Leadership Commitment: Visible prioritization
2. External Support: Bring in expertise if needed
3. Rapid Diagnosis: Root cause analysis within 1-2 weeks
4. 90-Day Plan: Specific, measurable actions with weekly tracking
5. Resource Surge: Additional budget/support for improvement
6. Communication: Transparent about situation and plan
7. Celebrate Progress: Mark improvements to maintain momentum

**All Recommendations Include**:
- Specific, concrete actions (not generic steps)
- Why this action targets the likely root cause
- Timeline (30/60/90 day priorities)
- Expected outcomes and metrics to track
- Resources needed
