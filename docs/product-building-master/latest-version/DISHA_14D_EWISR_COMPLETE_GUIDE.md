## Overview of 14-D EWISR Framework

### What is EWISR?

EWISR stands for **Educational Worth Institutional Strength Rating** - a comprehensive framework that assesses institutional health across 14 critical dimensions. It provides a holistic view of how well a school is functioning across all key areas.

### Why 14 Dimensions?

Educational research shows that institutional health is **multi-dimensional**. A school can be excellent in academics but weak in staff welfare, or have great infrastructure but poor parent engagement. The 14-D framework ensures no aspect is overlooked.

### The 14 Dimensions Organized by Impact

#### **Tier 1: Critical Success Factors (Weight: 10% each)**
These dimensions MUST be strong or school faces serious problems:
- **D01: Academic Reputation & Rigour** (10%) - Core mission of school
- **D03: Leadership & Governance** (10%) - Direction and decision-making
- **D05: Student Safety & Wellness** (10%) - Non-negotiable foundation

#### **Tier 2: Major Performance Drivers (Weight: 8-9% each)**
Strong performance here drives reputation and retention:
- **D02: Teacher Welfare & Development** (9%) - Best teaching requires happy teachers
- **D04: Parent Engagement & SLA** (8%) - Parent satisfaction = retention
- **D08: Individual Attention (PTR)** (9%) - Quality of instruction depends on class size
- **D12: Faculty Competence & Retention** (9%) - Teaching quality is everything
- **D14: Management Vision & Growth** (8%) - Sustainability and innovation

#### **Tier 3: Supporting Factors (Weight: 6-7% each)**
Important for competitive differentiation and long-term viability:
- **D06: Infrastructure & Facilities** (7%) - Environment matters
- **D09: Value for Money** (7%) - Financial sustainability
- **D11: Community Service & Responsibility** (5%) - Brand and values

#### **Tier 4: Specialization & Enhancement (Weight: 5-6% each)**
Differentiators for premium positioning:
- **D07: Co-Curricular Education** (6%) - Holistic development
- **D10: Special Needs Inclusivity** (6%) - Inclusive practices
- **D13: Internationalism & Cultural Diversity** (6%) - Global outlook

### Total Weight Distribution
```
Tier 1 (Critical): 30%
Tier 2 (Major Drivers): 43%
Tier 3 (Supporting): 19%
Tier 4 (Specialization): 17%
Total: 109% (normalized to 100% in calculation)
```

## Understanding Each Dimension

### D01: Academic Reputation & Rigour (Weight: 10%)

**Definition**: Perception and reality of academic quality, board exam performance, curriculum rigor, and institutional brand in academics.

**Why It Matters**:
- Core mission of educational institution
- Directly impacts parent choice and enrollment
- Foundation for everything else school does
- Board exam results are visible proof of quality

**Key Metrics**:
- Board exam pass rate vs national average
- Curriculum rigor vs peer schools
- % of high achievers (>70% aggregate)
- Community perception of academic standing

**Assessment Questions** (4 questions):
1. Board exam pass rate comparison
2. Curriculum rigor assessment
3. High achiever percentage
4. Community academic reputation

**Scoring**:
- 1-2: Excellent (>85% pass rate, above national average)
- 3-4: Good (75-85% pass rate, competitive)
- 5-6: Average (65-75% pass rate, at par)
- 7-8: Below Average (55-65% pass rate)
- 9-10: Poor (<55% pass rate)

**Benchmarks**:
- Excellent: 85/100
- Good: 60/100
- Average: 40/100
- Poor: 20/100

**Weight Reasoning**:
- 10% reflects that academics is core mission
- Not 20% because other factors also matter
- Research shows academic quality is #1 parent decision factor
- Directly linked to institutional brand and sustainability

## Weight System & Methodology

### Why These Weights?

The weights reflect:
1. **Impact on Learning Outcomes**: Higher weight if directly affects student learning
2. **Sustainability**: Higher weight if affects long-term viability
3. **Measurability**: Higher weight if objectively measurable
4. **Control**: Higher weight if school can directly control it
5. **Market Perception**: Higher weight if parents care about it

### Weight Distribution Rationale

```
Total Weight = 109%
Normalized to = 100% in calculations

Why >100%?
- Allows flexibility in weighting based on school context
- Reflects interdependence of dimensions
- Some schools can skip certain dimensions (e.g., international focus)
```

### Calculation Formula

```
Overall Health Index = Σ(Dimension Score × Dimension Weight) / Total Weight

Example:
D01 Score: 85 × Weight: 10% = 8.5
D02 Score: 75 × Weight: 9% = 6.75
...
D14 Score: 80 × Weight: 8% = 6.4

Sum of weighted scores / Sum of weights = Overall Index
```

## Questionnaire & Scoring System

### Question Structure

Each dimension has 4-5 assessment questions with:
- **Question**: Clear, specific, answerable
- **Ordinal Scale**: 1-10 scale with 5-6 options
- **Descriptive Labels**: Each weight has meaning (e.g., "Excellent", "Poor")
- **Measurability**: Based on observable metrics where possible

### 1-10 Scale Interpretation

```
Weight 1-2: Excellent
  - Top 10% schools
  - Best practice
  - No improvement needed

Weight 3-4: Good
  - Top 25-50% schools
  - Above average
  - Maintain and enhance

Weight 5-6: Average
  - Median school
  - At industry standard
  - Steady state

Weight 7-8: Poor
  - Bottom 25% schools
  - Below average
  - Needs improvement

Weight 9-10: Critical
  - Bottom 5% schools
  - Systemic issue
  - Urgent action required
```

### Converting Individual Scores to Dimension Score

**Step 1**: For each question, school selects one option (weight 1-10)

**Step 2**: Average all question weights for that dimension

```
Example: D01 with 4 questions
Q1 selected weight: 2 (Good)
Q2 selected weight: 4 (Average)
Q3 selected weight: 3 (Good)
Q4 selected weight: 2 (Excellent)

Average = (2+4+3+2)/4 = 2.75
```

**Step 3**: Convert 1-10 scale to 0-100 scale

```
Dimension Score = Average Weight × 10

Example:
2.75 × 10 = 27.5/100

Interpretation: Below Average (27.5 < 40 which is "average")
```

**Step 4**: Apply to weighted contribution

```
Weighted Contribution = (Dimension Score / 100) × Dimension Weight

Example:
(27.5 / 100) × 10% = 2.75% contribution to overall score
```

### Benchmark Comparison

Each dimension has benchmarks for comparison:

```
Example: D01 Benchmarks
Excellent: 85/100
Good: 60/100
Average: 40/100
Poor: 20/100
```

**Interpretation**:
```
If school scores 75/100 on D01:
- "Good" (75 > 60)
- Slightly below "Excellent" (75 < 85)
- Significantly above "Average" (75 > 40)

Recommendation: Maintain and work toward excellence (85+)
```

## Complete Calculation Flow

### Phase 1: Assessment Data Collection

**Step 1**: Gather stakeholder input
- Leadership team assessment
- Teacher surveys
- Parent feedback
- Student feedback (where age-appropriate)

**Step 2**: Answer all 4-5 questions for each dimension
- Select the most appropriate option for each question
- Record the weight (1-10) for that option

**Example**: Assessing D01 (Academic Reputation)
```
Q1.1: What is your board exam pass rate?
  School's answer: "85-95% pass rate (15-25% above national)"
  Selected Weight: 2

Q1.2: How rigorous is curriculum vs peers?
  School's answer: "Above average rigor - competitive edge"
  Selected Weight: 2

Q1.3: What % score above 70%?
  School's answer: "70-85% high achievers"
  Selected Weight: 2

Q1.4: Academic reputation?
  School's answer: "Well-known for good academics"
  Selected Weight: 2
```

### Phase 2: Dimension Score Calculation

**Step 3**: Calculate average for each dimension
```
D01 Average = (2 + 2 + 2 + 2) / 4 = 2.0
```

**Step 4**: Convert to 0-100 scale
```
D01 Score = 2.0 × 10 = 20/100

Wait, this seems low! Let me recalculate...

Actually, the logic is:
- Weight 1 = Excellent = 90-100 range
- Weight 2 = Very Good = 80-90 range
- Weight 3 = Good = 70-80 range
- Weight 4 = Average = 60-70 range
- Weight 5 = Below Avg = 40-60 range
- Weight 6+ = Poor = Below 40

Better conversion:
Score = 100 - (Average Weight - 1) × 10
D01 = 100 - (2.0 - 1) × 10 = 100 - 10 = 90/100

This is more accurate!
```

Actually, let me use the simpler inverse formula:
```
Dimension Score = 100 - (Average Weight × 10)

Because:
- Weight 1 → Score 90 (excellent)
- Weight 2 → Score 80 (very good)
- Weight 3 → Score 70 (good)
- Weight 4 → Score 60 (average)
- Weight 5 → Score 50 (below average)
- Weight 6+ → Score 40 (poor)

D01 Example:
Average = 2.0
Score = 100 - (2.0 × 10) = 80/100

Interpretation: VERY GOOD (80 = "very good" level)
```

### Phase 3: Weighted Aggregation

**Step 5**: Calculate weighted contribution for each dimension
```
D01 Contribution = (D01 Score / 100) × D01 Weight
                 = (80/100) × 10%
                 = 8.0%

This means D01 contributes 8 percentage points to overall score
(out of possible 10 percentage points max)
```

**Step 6**: Sum all weighted contributions

```
Example school's scores:
D01: 80 × 10% = 8.0 points
D02: 75 × 9% = 6.75 points
D03: 85 × 10% = 8.5 points
D04: 70 × 8% = 5.6 points
D05: 82 × 10% = 8.2 points
D06: 65 × 7% = 4.55 points
D07: 60 × 6% = 3.6 points
D08: 78 × 9% = 7.02 points
D09: 72 × 7% = 5.04 points
D10: 55 × 6% = 3.3 points
D11: 68 × 5% = 3.4 points
D12: 86 × 9% = 7.74 points
D13: 70 × 6% = 4.2 points
D14: 80 × 8% = 6.4 points

Total = 8.0+6.75+8.5+5.6+8.2+4.55+3.6+7.02+5.04+3.3+3.4+7.74+4.2+6.4
Total = 92.3 points (out of 109 possible)
```

### Phase 4: Normalize and Convert to 0-100 Scale

**Step 7**: Normalize to total possible weight (109%)
```
Total possible points = 109%
Actual points scored = 92.3%

Percentage = (92.3 / 109) × 100 = 84.7/100
```

### Phase 5: Determine Overall Health Status

**Step 8**: Classify based on final score
```
84.7/100 falls in "STRONG PERFORMER" range (70-85)

Classification:
90-100: ELITE EXCELLENCE - World class institutional health
80-89: STRONG PERFORMER - Well-managed institution
70-79: HEALTHY SCHOOL - Good foundation, areas to develop
60-69: AVERAGE PERFORMER - Mixed strengths and weaknesses
50-59: BELOW AVERAGE - Multiple significant gaps
<50: NEEDS SIGNIFICANT IMPROVEMENT - Critical issues
```

## Key Formulas Summary

### Dimension Score Formula
```
Dimension Score = 100 - (Average Question Weight × 10)

Why this formula?
- Weight 1 → Score 90 (excellent)
- Weight 2 → Score 80 (very good)
- Weight 3 → Score 70 (good)
- Weight 4 → Score 60 (average)
- Weight 5+ → Score 50- (below average/poor)
```

### Weighted Contribution Formula
```
Weighted Contribution = (Dimension Score / 100) × Dimension Weight

Example:
D01 Score 90 × Weight 10% = (90/100) × 10 = 9 percentage points
```

### Overall Health Index Formula
```
Overall Health Index = (Sum of Weighted Contributions / Sum of Weights) × 100

Example:
95.78 points / 109 total weight × 100 = 87.9/100
```
